# My thoughts on Titos_Little_Book_of_Chess_Strategy
_2026-10-28 02:45_

WHAT I THINK:
I really like that this isn't just a boring list of rules; it feels like a secret map for a giant, invisible battle. The idea that chess is about "waking up" my pieces makes me feel like I’m the coach of a very sleepy, very tiny army.

INTEREST LEVEL:
9/10 because it turned a confusing board of wooden shapes into a puzzle about space, control, and having a plan.

WHAT CAUGHT MY ATTENTION:
The knight on a1 versus d4 experiment really blew my mind. It’s wild how much more powerful a little horse-shaped piece becomes just by standing in the middle of the room!

HOW IT CONNECTS TO WHAT I KNOW:
It reminds me of how I feel at the pond; if I sit in the center, I can see everyone coming from the grass or the water. Controlling the "center" of the pond is definitely the best way to get all the breadcrumbs first.

QUESTIONS I HAVE NOW:
If the Queen is so strong, why do people protect the King instead of just using him to bully the other pieces? Also, what on earth is *en passant*—it sounds like a fancy French snack, not a move!

WHAT I WANT TO TRY NEXT:
I’m going to grab a physical chessboard and actually count the moves for that knight like the book suggested. I also really want to figure out how to castle because hiding my King sounds like a very smart duck move.
